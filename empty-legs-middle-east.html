The Smart Way to Fly Private

What Is a Middle East Empty Leg?

An empty leg flight is a private jet repositioning after a one-way charter — for example, a jet returning to Dubai after dropping passengers in Riyadh. Instead of flying that leg empty, operators list it at a significant discount, giving travelers a rare chance to fly private for a fraction of the standard charter cost. The trade-off is flexibility: you take the route, date, and time the aircraft is already flying, rather than setting your own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Empty legs in the Middle East work just like anywhere else: a private jet is repositioning after a one-way charter (e.g., returning to Dubai from Riyadh) and is sold at a discount to avoid flying empty. You get the same luxury aircraft and crew for a fraction of the standard charter cost.

Yes. We frequently see empty legs originating from, or arriving into, Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai, Doha, and Abu Dhabi, especially along high-frequency domestic and Gulf corridors.

Empty legs in the Middle East typically sell at 30–75% below standard charter rates, depending on the route, aircraft type, and how close to departure you book. A Dubai–Riyadh light jet that normally charters for $15,000–25,000 may be available as an empty leg for $5,000–10,000. Dubai–London heavy jets frequently drop from $100,000+ to $50,000–70,000.

Most empty legs become available 24–72 hours before departure, with some listed several weeks out. The sweet spot for finding them is within two weeks of travel, when operators finalise their schedules. If you have flexibility, registering for alerts on your preferred routes is the most effective strategy.

No. The departure and destination airports, as well as the departure time, are fixed by the operator's repositioning schedule. Empty legs are single-leg flights, not flexible charters. If you need a specific time or routing, a standard charter or multi-leg quote is the correct option.

Yes. Empty leg flights operate under the same regulatory framework, crew certification, and maintenance standards as any commercial charter. The aircraft and crew are identical to a standard charter — the only difference is the commercial positioning of the leg.

Because empty legs are linked to a primary charter, they can occasionally shift or cancel at short notice if the preceding flight changes. Reputable brokers will notify you immediately and, where possible, offer an alternative empty leg or a discounted standard charter. Cancellation and rebooking terms are confirmed in writing before payment.

The UAE and Saudi Arabia are the most active corridors, with frequent repositioning between Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, and Jeddah. Doha and Kuwait City also generate steady business-aviation traffic. Seasonal peaks occur during Ramadan, Hajj, and the winter tourism season.

Yes, but baggage capacity is subject to the aircraft's payload limits and the operator's policy for that specific leg. Light jets typically accommodate 6–8 passengers with standard luggage; heavy jets handle considerably more. Oversized or excess baggage should be declared at the enquiry stage so the operator can confirm.

A standard charter is booked around your schedule, route, and aircraft preference. An empty leg is a fixed, one-way repositioning flight already scheduled by the operator, sold at a discount to avoid flying empty. The trade-off is flexibility: you accept the operator's route, date, and time rather than setting your own.
