Live Animal Charter: How Racehorses, Livestock, and Working Animals Move by Air

Moving live animals by air is one of cargo aviation's most specialized disciplines — governed by a dedicated IATA manual, dependent on species-specific handling, and demanding everything from purpose-built jet stalls to in-flight veterinary supervision. The clearest example at scale is the Gulf's thoroughbred racing circuit, where charter flights move hundreds of horses a season under conditions commercial cargo simply cannot provide.

By the Skylinks Asia Charter Desk · Reviewed by our Operations Team · Published October 2026

TL;DR: Live animal air cargo — racehorses, livestock, zoo and conservation animals, and pets — is governed by IATA's Live Animals Regulations (LAR), a dedicated manual covering container specifications, documentation, and species-specific handling, separate from both DGR and PCR. The clearest example of this category's scale and complexity is the Gulf's thoroughbred racing circuit: Dubai's Meydan Racecourse alone brought 101 horses from 17 countries to the 2026 Dubai World Cup, flown in climate-controlled jet stalls with flying grooms in attendance, under veterinary and quarantine protocols managed for weeks before the race itself.

What Does IATA's Live Animals Regulations (LAR) Actually Cover?

The IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR), maintained by the IATA Live Animals & Perishables Board, is the global standard for air-transporting live animals of every kind — pets, racehorses, zoo and conservation animals, livestock, and laboratory or agricultural animals. It governs four core areas:

  1. Container specifications by species — from standard pet carriers through purpose-built jet stalls for horses.
  2. Government and carrier-specific regulations by country — including import permits and disease-control requirements.
  3. Documentation requirements — shipper's certification, dedicated air waybill, and captain notification of species, location, and quantity of live cargo aboard.
  4. Species-specific handling — temperature, ventilation, feeding, and rest intervals during transit.

One rule worth knowing: live animals must not be entered on the same air waybill as other commodities, regardless of how the shipment is otherwise consolidated. For temperature-sensitive live cargo the handling requirement runs parallel to pharma and cold chain charter protocols, though the applicable rulebook is LAR, not IATA's Temperature Control Regulations.

IATA has also launched LAR Verify, a digital portal giving airlines, shippers, and freight forwarders real-time access to operator- and destination-specific requirements, including live updates on regional embargoes and disease-outbreak-related restrictions — a meaningful improvement over manually cross-checking country-by-country rules for every shipment.

How Do Racehorses Actually Fly to the Gulf's Racing Circuit?

Nowhere does live animal air charter operate at greater scale or stakes than the Gulf's thoroughbred racing calendar. The Dubai World Cup, held at Meydan Racecourse, marked its 30th edition in 2026 with a $30.5 million prize purse across nine races and 101 horses from 17 countries competing. The Saudi Cup, one of the world's richest horse races, has relied on dedicated charter flights to bring horses in from as far as Los Angeles and Miami.

The logistics behind this are genuinely specialized. Horses travel in purpose-built jet stalls — up to 25 per aircraft, with up to three horses per stall — fitted with rubberized flooring, foam padding, and hay nets, in cargo holds held at a steady 12-15°C. Widebody freighters such as the Boeing 777 are commonly configured for this kind of mission. Professional flying grooms travel with the horses throughout. On arrival, horses move directly into mandated quarantine stabling — Dubai Racing Club operates dedicated quarantine facilities near the airport specifically for this purpose — for a period set by their country of origin, well before they are cleared to train or race.

2026 demonstrated exactly why charter flexibility matters in this category: when regional airspace closures disrupted the usual routing, operators arranged a four-hour climate-controlled road transfer from Doha to Riyadh so horses could fly onward from Saudi Arabia instead, where airspace remained open — preserving the horses' condition and getting them to Dubai in time for the 30th Dubai World Cup to proceed as scheduled. It's a concrete illustration of the same principle covered in our repatriation and government charter guide: charter's ability to reroute around regional disruption is exactly what scheduled service often cannot offer.

What Other Live Animals Move by Air Charter?

Beyond headline racing events, live animal charter also moves:

  • Breeding livestock and genetics — pedigree cattle, dairy breeding stock, and other agricultural animals requiring quarantine-compliant transport.
  • Zoo and conservation animals — relocating between institutions, often under species-specific veterinary supervision and CITES oversight.
  • Working and competition animals — police, military, and service animals, plus competition animals outside the racing circuit.

Each is governed by the same LAR container and documentation framework, adjusted for species-specific needs around temperature, ventilation, feeding, and rest intervals during transit. What Are the CITES Requirements for Protected Species?

Any live animal shipment involving a CITES-listed (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) species requires the relevant export and import CITES permits in addition to standard LAR documentation. These requirements exist specifically to prevent illegal wildlife trade, and they apply regardless of the animal's destination or stated purpose.
