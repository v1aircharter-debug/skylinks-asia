Empty Leg Flights from Dubai to Europe: Routes, Prices & How to Book

By the Skylinks Asia Charter Desk

Published 3 November 2026 · Approx. 9 min read

Typical routes include London, Paris, Frankfurt, Munich, Milan, Istanbul, and Newcastle, with indicative prices from roughly $18,000 to $55,000 depending on aircraft class and route length. What Are Empty Leg Flights and Why Do They Exist?

An empty leg is a repositioning flight — a private jet that must fly one way without paying passengers, usually to return to its base or to pick up the next charter. Rather than fly empty, the operator sells the leg at a steep discount, typically 50 to 75 percent below a standard one-way charter. It is a real commercial flight on a real AOC-certified aircraft; the savings come from the operator's need to reduce the cost of the ferry, not from any reduction in service or safety.

Dubai is one of the highest-volume empty-leg corridors in the world because the region's private jet traffic is highly directional. Peak season brings inbound jets from Europe, Africa, and Asia full of passengers; the outbound legs are frequently empty. The result is a steady flow of Dubai-to-Europe empty legs — particularly between October and March, when inbound demand peaks and repositioning opportunities accumulate. How Much Do Empty Leg Flights from Dubai to Europe Cost?

The empty-leg discount is the entire point of the product, and it varies by aircraft class. As a rule of thumb, a Dubai-to-Europe empty leg prices at roughly 25 to 50 percent of the equivalent one-way charter on the same aircraft — because the operator is recovering a fraction of the ferry cost, not pricing the aircraft as a dedicated charter. Three drivers determine where in the band you land:

  1. Aircraft class. A light jet (which cannot cover Dubai to Europe on range anyway) would be cheapest if it could; among the classes that can, midsize jets are the most affordable and ultra-long-range jets the most expensive. Expect roughly $18,000–$30,000 for a midsize on a shorter route, $25,000–$45,000 for a heavy on a mid-length route, and $40,000–$55,000 for an ultra-long-range on the longest routes.
  2. Notice period. A leg departing in under 24 hours frequently prices below market because the operator's alternative is to fly empty. Legs offered 5–14 days out price closer to the standard discount band — the operator has time to attract competing interest.
  3. Season and direction. October to March — when Europe-to-Dubai inbound demand peaks — produces the most Dubai-to-Europe empty legs and the deepest discounts. Summer months (June–August) are thinner on this corridor; empty legs shift to other routes.

How Do You Find and Book an Empty Leg Flight from Dubai?

Because empty legs are short-notice and change hourly, the process is different from a standard charter. Four steps work reliably:

  1. Register for alerts. Sign up on our empty leg alerts page with your preferred route (e.g. Dubai → London) and travel window. We notify registered clients first when matching legs appear — typically before they reach public aggregators.
  2. Check the live hub regularly. If you're flexible, check the Dubai empty-legs hub and the Middle East regional page at the top of each week. Legs appear 48 hours to 14 days out, so a weekly check catches most supply.
  3. Confirm the operator and aircraft before you pay. Ask for the AOC holder's name and the aircraft registration. An empty leg is a real flight — you can verify it independently, and you should. Our broker-vetting guide walks through the checks.
  4. Accept the fixed terms and pay. Empty legs are non-refundable and the departure date cannot be moved. Payment goes to the operator or broker trust account, not to a personal account. Contract is signed, trip brief is issued, and the aircraft is yours.

What Aircraft Are Typically Used on Dubai-Europe Empty Legs?

Dubai to Europe is a 4.5–8 hour flight, which eliminates light jets from the mix entirely — their 1,200–1,500 nm range falls short of the 2,500–3,500 nm needed. Three classes cover the corridor:

  • Midsize jets — Challenger 350, Citation XLS+, Hawker 850XP, Falcon 2000LXS. Best on shorter routes: Dubai to Istanbul, Vienna, Athens. 8–10 seats, 20–30 cu ft baggage.
  • Heavy jets — Challenger 605, Falcon 900EX, Legacy 600, Gulfstream G450. Comfortable on Dubai to Frankfurt, Munich, Milan, Rome, Paris. 10–14 seats, 100+ cu ft baggage.
  • Ultra-long-range jets — Global 6000, Gulfstream G650ER, Falcon 7X. Needed for Dubai to London, Newcastle, or Geneva with full payload. 12–16 seats, full berthing, largest baggage capacity.

What Should You Watch Out For When Booking an Empty Leg?

Five points differentiate a smooth empty-leg booking from a costly one:

  • Non-refundable. Empty-leg pricing assumes the flight goes. If your plans change, you do not get the money back — and most contracts include no rebooking rights.
  • Fixed departure date. Unlike a charter, an empty leg cannot move. If your travel window is inflexible, price a standard one-way charter instead.
  • Limited baggage. Empty legs often come with the aircraft's standard empty-leg allowance, not the charter allowance. Confirm before booking if you're carrying more than a standard suitcase per passenger.
  • Operator verification. As with any charter, verify the AOC holder and aircraft registration independently — the empty-leg discount is not a discount on safety, but it is occasionally offered by operators you would not want to fly.
  • Hidden fees. De-icing, VIP catering, ground transport, and any route change after confirmation are usually quoted separately. Ask for a fully itemised price before signing.
