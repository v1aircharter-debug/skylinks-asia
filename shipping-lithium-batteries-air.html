The Ultimate Guide to Shipping Lithium Batteries by Air

August 2024•Skylinks Asia Team

With the global boom in electric vehicles (EVs) and consumer electronics, the demand for shipping lithium batteries by air has skyrocketed. However, lithium batteries are classified as Class 9 Dangerous Goods (DGR) due to their fire risk.

Why are Lithium Batteries Dangerous in Air Freight?

Lithium-ion and lithium-metal batteries can experience thermal runaway—a chemical reaction that causes intense heat and self-igniting fires. These fires are extremely difficult to extinguish in an aircraft cargo hold, making proper packaging, segregation, and handling critical.

IATA Regulations for DGR Cargo

The International Air Transport Association (IATA) updates its Dangerous Goods Regulations annually. Shippers must ensure batteries are tested, packaged, and labeled according to UN specifications. State and operator variations may also apply, requiring expert coordination to prevent cargo being rejected at the airport.

Benefits of a Dedicated DGR Cargo Charter

For high-volume battery shipments, a dedicated cargo charter offers a compliant and efficient solution. It allows for direct routing, specialized fire-suppression pallets, and immediate handling, minimizing the time cargo spends in transit or storage.
