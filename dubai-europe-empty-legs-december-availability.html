Empty Leg Availability — December 2026

Dubai to Europe Empty Legs:
December 2026 Availability

The Dubai–Europe corridor sees its highest empty-leg volume of the year between 15 December and 5 January. Here is how the December 2026 window is shaping up across eleven European destinations.

TL;DR: December empty legs on the Dubai–Europe corridor cluster in two windows — 15–21 December (pre-Christmas return wave) and 22–28 December (between Christmas and New Year). Eleven European destinations see regular repositioning volume: London, Paris, Frankfurt, Munich, Newcastle, Istanbul, Amsterdam, Zurich, Geneva, Milan, and Rome. Zurich and Geneva see a December surge from ski-season repositioning. Empty legs go live 48 hours to 7 days before flight, are sold as whole-aircraft one-way movements, and are claimed within 4–12 hours of listing. Register for VIP alerts in mid-November to catch the Christmas window.

When are the best weeks to catch Dubai–Europe empty legs in December 2026?

Weeks 3 and 4 — 15–21 December and 22–28 December — carry the highest empty-leg volume of the year on the Dubai–Europe corridor. The pre-Christmas window (15–21 December) sees the largest return wave, as European holidaymakers fly home from Dubai on repositioning aircraft. The between-Christmas-and-New-Year window (22–28 December) carries a second wave driven by corporate year-end movements and fleet repositioning for the New Year.

Week 1 (1–7 December) and Week 2 (8–14 December) are comparatively quiet. Early December is still corporate season, and aircraft are committed to scheduled charter work before the holiday wave. Empty legs appear sporadically — typically one to three a week per route — rather than in the concentrated clusters seen mid-month.

Week 5 (29 December – 4 January) is a mixed window. New Year return traffic generates secondary repositioning volume, but many operators have already completed their European repositioning by 30 December. The window is narrower and seats are claimed faster.

What aircraft types typically operate Dubai–Europe empty legs?

Four aircraft classes dominate Dubai–Europe empty legs in December: midsize, super-midsize, heavy, and ultra-long-range. Light jets appear occasionally on Dubai–Istanbul and other shorter segments, but the Dubai–Europe distance (typically 3,000–5,500 nautical miles) rules out most light jets for direct routing.

How does a December empty leg differ from a one-way charter?

An empty leg is a repositioning flight sold at a discount because the aircraft has to fly empty anyway. A one-way charter is a full-fare booking where the operator positions an aircraft specifically for your flight. The distinction matters most in December because the corridor sees both in high volume.

Empty legs are inherently one-way, cheaper than a one-way charter, and constrained by the operator's schedule. A one-way charter gives you full control over departure time and aircraft selection, but you pay for the aircraft to fly to your origin airport and back to its base. In December, empty legs on the Dubai–Europe corridor offer the best value for travelers whose dates align with the repositioning windows.

The trade-off is flexibility. An empty leg departs when the aircraft must move — not when you want to leave. For December travelers whose dates are fixed, a one-way charter or a scheduled business-class booking may be the more practical option. For travelers with some flexibility around the 15–28 December window, the empty-leg corridor offers significant savings on private jet charter.

How far in advance should I book a December empty leg?

December empty legs are booked late by nature. The underlying charter is confirmed first; the empty leg is listed once the operator knows the aircraft must reposition. That means most December empty legs appear on the market 48 hours to 7 days before flight.

  1. Register by mid-November. Set up an empty-leg alert for the Dubai–Europe corridor by 15 November. The first Christmas-week listings appear in late November, so you want to be on the list before they go live.
  2. Respond within 2 hours of an alert. Empty legs are claimed on a first-come, first-served basis. Most disappear within 4–12 hours. A slow response means missing the seat.
  3. Confirm operator details before payment. Ask for the operator's AOC, aircraft registration, and safety rating. Reputable brokers disclose these within an hour of enquiry.
  4. Accept the departure time as listed. Empty legs do not shift. If the aircraft departs at 04:00 on 22 December, that is the flight. Flexibility on arrival date matters more than flexibility on departure time.
  5. Book return travel separately. Empty legs are one-way by definition. Return flights on the same corridor are far rarer; return travel is either a second empty leg if available or a one-way charter at standard rates.

How do I vet an operator when booking a December empty leg?

Three checks matter before you pay for a December empty leg: the operator's AOC and registration, the aircraft's safety rating, and the crew's hours on type. Ask for all three in writing.

The operator's AOC (Air Operator Certificate) confirms the carrier is licensed to operate the flight commercially. Cross-check the certificate number against the country of registration's civil aviation authority database. The aircraft's safety rating — ARGUS, Wyvern, or IS-BAO — provides an independent assessment of the operator's maintenance and crew training standards. Crew hours on type matter more in December than in other months because winter weather adds risk to the Dubai–Europe sector.

For a full vetting checklist, see business aviation safety ratings and how to vet a charter broker. A reputable broker provides these details as part of the standard booking process — not as a special request.

Why do empty legs appear on the Dubai–Europe corridor in December?

Four demand drivers generate December empty legs on the Dubai–Europe corridor: holiday return traffic, corporate year-end movements, ski season repositioning, and fleet repositioning for Q1.

The holiday return wave is the largest driver. Europeans who flew into Dubai for December holidays return home on aircraft that operators need to reposition anyway. The return leg is sold as an empty leg because it would otherwise fly empty.

Corporate year-end movements add a second layer. Companies host December events and board meetings in Dubai, then send executives home on aircraft that must reposition. Q4 incentive programs also drive volume.

The ski season is a third driver specific to Zurich and Geneva. Aircraft positioned to Dubai for winter-sun charters must return to Swiss and French bases for ski-season bookings starting in mid-December. That repositioning creates the corridor's highest-value empty legs.

Fleet repositioning for Q1 adds a final layer in the last week of December. Operators move aircraft to European bases for January corporate traffic, and those repositioning flights appear as empty legs.
