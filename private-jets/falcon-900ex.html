Aircraft Profile

Dassault Falcon 900EX

Entering service in 1996, the Dassault Falcon 900EX is the extended-range evolution of the Falcon 900B — the aircraft that established Dassault's three-engine, wide-cabin formula as a transatlantic standard. The EX upgrades the airframe with Honeywell TFE731-60 engines (5,000 pounds of thrust each), a modern glass cockpit, and increased fuel capacity for 4,500 nautical miles of range with eight passengers. The three-engine configuration is the defining feature: it delivers exceptional overwater safety margins without ETOPS constraints, steeper second-segment climb gradients from hot-and-high airports, and short-field performance that lets the 900EX operate from runways most heavy jets cannot — Florence, London City, Aspen, and St. Moritz are all within its capability. The cabin is 33 feet long, 7 feet 8 inches wide, and 6 feet 2 inches high, typically configured in three distinct zones: a forward conference or club area, a mid-cabin dining and divan zone, and an aft stateroom with an enclosed lavatory. The 127 cubic feet of external baggage capacity is externally accessible and comfortably handles 12 to 14 passengers on multi-day transatlantic trips. In charter service, the 900EX is the aircraft you choose when the mission requires transatlantic range but not the full 6,000 nm of the 7X or 8X, or when the departure or arrival airport has restrictive runway or climb requirements. It competes directly with the Challenger 605 and the Gulfstream G450, and its three-engine safety margin and short-field agility remain respected across corporate flight departments in Europe, the Middle East, and Asia.

Falcon 900EX FAQs

The Dassault Falcon 900EX is classified as a Heavy Jet. It entered service in 1996 as the extended-range evolution of the Falcon 900B, featuring upgraded TFE731-60 engines, improved avionics, and 4,500 nautical miles of range on the proven three-engine Falcon airframe.

The Falcon 900EX has a maximum range of 4,500 nautical miles with eight passengers. This supports non-stop transatlantic missions such as Paris to New York, Dubai to London, and Singapore to Tokyo. It sits between the Challenger 605 (4,000 nm) and the Falcon 7X (5,950 nm).

The Falcon 900EX is certified for up to 19 passengers and is typically configured for 12 to 14 in a standard three-zone cabin layout. Cabin layouts vary by operator, with many configured for 12 passengers in a conference/dining/divan arrangement with a forward galley and aft lavatory.

The Falcon 900EX is powered by three Honeywell TFE731-60 turbofan engines, each producing approximately 5,000 pounds of thrust. The three-engine configuration offers exceptional overwater safety margins, steeper climb gradients from challenging airports, and short-field performance that few heavy jets can match.

Yes. The Falcon 900EX has a flat-floor stand-up cabin with 6 feet 2 inches of headroom, 7 feet 8 inches of width, and 33 feet of length — approximately 6 feet shorter than the Falcon 7X but with the same cross-section. The three-zone cabin is typically configured with a forward conference area, mid-cabin dining and divan, and an aft stateroom.

The Falcon 900EX offers 127 cubic feet of external baggage space, externally accessible from the rear. This comfortably accommodates 12 to 14 passengers on multi-day transatlantic trips with golf bags or ski equipment, and is 12 cubic feet larger than the Challenger 605.

Charter costs for a Falcon 900EX typically range from $11,500 to $14,000 per hour, depending on route, region, and season. The Falcon 900LX is a 2010 upgrade of the Falcon 900EX. It introduces winglets (reducing fuel burn by 5–7 percent), upgraded EASy II avionics, and improved short-field performance. Range is broadly similar at 4,750 nautical miles, but the LX offers better efficiency and lower operating costs on identical missions.
