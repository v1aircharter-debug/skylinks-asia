Rig Moves & Heavy Air Freight

Keeping the Energy Sector Running

Energy infrastructure is often located in remote, harsh environments with limited ground infrastructure. Whether you are executing a planned rig move, replacing a critical refinery valve, or transporting oversized drilling machinery, we source the right freighter aircraft for the job. From ramp-loading turboprops for desert airstrips to widebody freighters for turbine engines, we ensure your energy cargo arrives safely and on schedule.

Quick Answer

Oil and gas equipment charter moves drilling and production equipment directly to remote fields or offshore support bases that sea freight can't reach fast enough. Aircraft choice depends on cargo shape: ramp-loading freighters (An-12, IL-76) handle heavy awkward equipment; large-door regional freighters (ATR 72F) serve shorter strips; and outsize aircraft (An-124) step in for complete modular units. Most charters can be arranged within 24-48 hours.

Cargo Scope

What Equipment Moves on Energy Charter Flights?

Drilling Equipment

Drill bits, drill pipe and tubulars, mud pumps, and blowout preventers (BOPs), often needed on-site within days of a failure to avoid an extended stoppage.

Production & Processing Machinery

Compressors, generators, turbines, wellheads, and subsea trees, frequently weighing tens of tonnes per unit.

Pipeline Infrastructure

Large-diameter pipe segments, industrial valves, and specialized welding equipment for construction and repair projects in areas with limited ground access.

Regulated Chemicals & Materials

Drilling fluids, completion chemicals, and lubricants that are frequently classified as dangerous goods and require compliant handling.

Aircraft Selection

Which Aircraft Handle Oil & Gas Transport?

Ramp-Loading Freighters

Antonov An-12, Ilyushin IL-76

True rear-ramp aircraft built for driving heavy, awkward equipment straight on board. The standard choice for drilling tools, pipeline valves, and rig components too heavy or bulky for a standard side-door freighter.

Large-Door Regional Freighters

ATR 72F, BAe 146-200F

Side-loading aircraft suited to shorter desert or bush airstrips near smaller field sites, where a full ramp-loader either can't land or isn't needed for the load size.

Outsize Freighters

Antonov An-124

Reserved for complete modular process units or single pieces too large for anything else; specialist heavy-lift operators have based dedicated An-124s specifically to serve major oil and gas hubs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of equipment can be moved by oil and gas air charter?

Drilling tools (drill bits, tubulars, mud pumps, blowout preventers), production machinery (compressors, generators, turbines, wellheads), pipeline infrastructure (large-diameter pipe, valves), refinery turnaround parts, and regulated chemicals used in drilling and completion operations.

How fast can an oil and gas equipment charter be arranged?

Most charters can be arranged within 24 to 48 hours of the request, depending on aircraft availability and route. Genuine emergency shutdown situations, where a facility is losing revenue by the hour, are typically prioritized for faster dispatch.

Why charter an aircraft instead of shipping oilfield equipment by sea?

Sea freight for a critical component can take weeks — time an active drilling or production operation often can't absorb. A charter can deliver the same equipment directly to the nearest suitable airstrip in a day or two, which is frequently worth the cost premium when the alternative is extended downtime.

Can charter aircraft reach remote desert airstrips and offshore support bases?

Yes. Ramp-loading freighters and regional turboprop freighters are specifically chosen for their ability to operate from short, unpaved, or otherwise limited-infrastructure airstrips near field sites, which scheduled cargo services generally can't serve.

What's the difference between an An-12/IL-76 charter and an An-124 charter for oil and gas cargo?

The An-12 and IL-76 are ramp-loading freighters suited to heavy but conventionally-sized drilling equipment. The An-124 is reserved for complete modular units or single pieces too large or heavy for either of those aircraft — full process skids, large generators, or oversized structural components.

Does oil and gas charter cargo require dangerous goods handling?

Often, yes. Drilling fluids, completion chemicals, and various lubricants used in oilfield operations are frequently classified as dangerous goods under IATA regulations, and some require Cargo Aircraft Only handling depending on classification and quantity.

What drives the cost of an oil and gas equipment charter?

Aircraft type and size, cargo weight and dimensions, distance to the destination airstrip, any positioning flight needed to bring the aircraft to origin, and urgency all factor into cost — a planned rig move typically costs less than an emergency shutdown dispatch on the same route.
