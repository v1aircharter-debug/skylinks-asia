Overview

Aircraft Profile

The Ilyushin Il-76 family (including the original Il-76T and the upgraded Il-76TD) represents the pinnacle of rugged, strategic heavy-lift aviation. Originally designed for military operations, these four-engine freighters are heavily utilized in civilian logistics to access some of the most remote and underdeveloped regions on earth.

Capable of carrying up to 50 metric tons of payload, the Il-76 excels in humanitarian relief, oil and gas logistics, and mining operations. Its most defining feature is its ability to land on unpaved, gravel, or ice runways thanks to its robust multi-wheel landing gear. Equipped with a rear ramp, heavy machinery can be driven directly into the aircraft, eliminating the need for specialized ground handling equipment at destination airports. The Il-76TD variant offers increased payload, better fuel efficiency, and enhanced performance for extreme weather and high-altitude airports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Il-76 & Il-76TD Charter Queries

The Il-76T is the original civilian cargo variant. The Il-76TD (Transportnyy Dahl'niy - long-range transport) is an upgraded version with increased payload capacity (up to 50 tons), better fuel efficiency, reinforced wings, and improved performance for extreme weather and high-altitude airports.

The Ilyushin Il-76TD has a maximum payload capacity of approximately 50,000 kg (50 metric tons or 110,000 pounds), making it a highly capable strategic heavy-lift freighter for outsized and dense cargo.

Yes, the Il-76 family is specifically designed with robust, multi-wheel landing gear and low-pressure tires to operate from unpaved, gravel, ice, or dirt strips, making it essential for delivering cargo to remote, underdeveloped regions.

At maximum payload (50 tons), the Il-76TD has a range of approximately 4,200 kilometers (2,269 nautical miles). With lighter payloads, it can fly significantly further, making it ideal for regional heavy-lift logistics.

The Il-76 features a large rear ramp and clamshell doors, allowing vehicles and heavy machinery to be driven or winched straight into the cargo hold. It is also equipped with internal ceiling winches and monorails to easily maneuver non-palletized cargo, minimizing ground handling equipment requirements.

The Il-76 typically requires a runway of approximately 1,700 to 2,000 meters at maximum takeoff weight on paved surfaces. On unpaved or gravel strips, longer distances and reduced payloads are required. Runway bearing strength and surface condition are verified during pre-flight planning before any charter is confirmed.

The Il-76 is optimised for outsize, dense, and non-palletised freight: construction and mining machinery, oil and gas equipment, industrial vehicles, humanitarian relief supplies, and bulk cargo to remote airstrips. It is less economical for light, palletised, or containerised freight where a 737-800BCF or 767-300F would be more cost-effective.

The Il-76TD cargo hold measures approximately 20 meters long, 3.4 meters wide, and 3.4 meters high, giving roughly 186 cubic meters of usable volume across the main compartment. The rear ramp opening is wide enough to drive standard vehicles and palletised loads directly into the hold.

Il-76 charter costs typically range from $18,000 to $30,000 per block hour depending on route, payload, season, and aircraft positioning.
