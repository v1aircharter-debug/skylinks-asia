Overview

Aircraft Profile

The Boeing 777-300 is the stretched variant of the highly successful 777 family, designed to offer maximum passenger capacity while maintaining exceptional long-haul efficiency. As a twin-engine widebody, it provides a highly economical alternative to four-engine aircraft like the 747, without compromising on range or comfort.

With the ability to carry up to 550 passengers over 11,165 kilometers, the 777-300 is the premier choice for high-density international group travel, religious pilgrimage charters, and large sports team delegations. Its massive twin-aisle cabin provides a spacious and comfortable environment, while its enormous belly cargo capacity easily handles extensive luggage and equipment requirements. It is also a highly sought-after platform for VIP government conversions (BBJ), offering unparalleled cabin volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

777-300 Charter Queries

How many passengers can the Boeing 777-300 hold?

In a typical single-class charter configuration, the Boeing 777-300 can comfortably accommodate up to 550 passengers. In a multi-class layout, it typically holds between 350 and 400 passengers, making it ideal for massive international groups and pilgrimage flights.

What is the maximum range of a chartered Boeing 777-300?

The Boeing 777-300 has a maximum range of approximately 11,165 kilometers (6,024 nautical miles), allowing for up to 13 hours of continuous flight. This enables non-stop travel between major intercontinental hubs.

What is the difference between the Boeing 777-200 and the 777-300?

The 777-300 is a stretched version of the 777-200. It is over 10 meters (33 feet) longer, offering significantly more passenger and cargo capacity. While the -200 is highly efficient, the -300 is preferred when moving the maximum number of passengers on a single long-haul flight.

Is the Boeing 777-300 suitable for VIP or government charters?

Yes, the 777-300 is frequently utilized as a Boeing Business Jet (BBJ). Its massive cabin volume allows for highly customized VIP interiors, including luxury lounges, private offices, and bedrooms, making it a favorite for heads of state and large diplomatic delegations.

What kind of runways does the 777-300 require for charter flights?

As a large commercial widebody jet, the Boeing 777-300 requires paved, standardized runways typically at least 2,500 to 3,000 meters (8,200 to 10,000 feet) in length. It is designed to operate primarily from major international airports.
