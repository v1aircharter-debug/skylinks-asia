Common Questions

Tokyo ↔ Seoul FAQ

An empty leg on the Tokyo ↔ Seoul corridor is a private jet repositioning sector — the aircraft has just dropped passengers at one end and must return or relocate to the other without a paying load. Because the operator still has to move the aircraft, these sectors are typically listed 25–75% below the standard one-way charter rate. Skylinks Asia lists live availability on this corridor as it is released by our operator network.
Typical savings on Tokyo to Seoul empty legs range from 45–65% off the standard one-way charter rate. The discount depends on aircraft type, direction of travel, and lead time to departure. Because the corridor carries heavy bidirectional corporate and government traffic, both eastbound (NRT/HND → ICN/GMP) and westbound (ICN/GMP → NRT/HND) sectors list frequently at short notice.
The great-circle distance from Tokyo Narita (RJAA) to Seoul Incheon (RKSI) is approximately 670 nautical miles / 1,240 km. Typical block time is 1 hour 55 minutes to 2 hours 20 minutes depending on departure and arrival airport pairs, jet type, and airway routing. Light and midsize jets complete the sector non-stop with full reserves; larger aircraft often continue onward to China, Southeast Asia, or Europe without refuelling.
In Tokyo, private jets use Narita (RJAA) or Haneda (RJTT), with Haneda's dedicated business aviation terminal preferred for time-critical missions despite slot pressure. In Seoul, private jets arrive at Incheon (RKSI) or Gimpo (RKSS), the latter favoured for central-city convenience. Both capitals require advance slot and parking coordination, particularly during peak corporate periods and around major regional events.
The TYO ↔ SEL corridor sees a full spectrum of business jets. Light and midsize aircraft such as the Citation XLS+ and Hawker 850XP cover the sector efficiently with 6–8 passengers. Super-midsize jets including the Challenger 350 and Legacy 500 are the most common for 8–9 passengers. Heavy and ultra-long-range aircraft such as the Challenger 605, Global 6000, Global 7500, and Gulfstream G650ER handle 12–14 passengers and frequently continue onward to long-haul destinations.
Empty legs on the Tokyo ↔ Seoul corridor typically sell within a few hours of listing. Because the flight is short and both cities host dense corporate and diplomatic traffic, position-hungry operators release these sectors late and price them to move fast.
