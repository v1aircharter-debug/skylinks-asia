How Much Does It Cost to Charter a Private Jet or Cargo Plane?

By the Skylinks Asia Charter Desk · Reviewed by our Operations Team · October 2026

TL;DR: There is no fixed rate card for aircraft charter — pricing is quoted per mission, not per hour or per kilogram, because every flight combines a different aircraft, route, and set of ground costs. As a rough 2026 guide, private jet charter runs from about $1,500/hour for a turboprop up to $20,000+/hour for an ultra-long-range jet. Cargo charter runs from about $1,500–$6,000/hour for a small feeder aircraft up to $70,000–$110,000/hour for an Antonov An-124 outsize freighter, with most mid-range narrowbody freighter charters landing in the $8,000–$28,000/hour band. The aircraft category sets the baseline; positioning, permits, handling, and timing move the final number from there.

Why is there no fixed charter rate card?

Scheduled airlines publish fares because they fly the same route on the same aircraft every day. Charter does not work that way. Every charter mission is quoted individually because it combines a specific aircraft, a specific routing (often including an empty positioning leg to reach you), specific airport and handling costs at both ends, and specific timing — demand on a given route on a given date moves the price as much as the aircraft itself does.

That is why charter brokers quote a mission price, not a per-kilo or per-seat rate: the quote covers aircraft hire, crew, fuel, navigation and overflight permits, and ground handling at both ends as one number, not a menu of separate line items the way scheduled freight or airline tickets are priced.

How much does private jet charter cost by aircraft category?

Private jet charter is usually quoted — and shopped by buyers — as an hourly rate, since passenger charter missions tend to be simpler point-to-point trips without the ground-handling complexity cargo charter involves. 2026 industry pricing guides converge on roughly these bands:

Ultra-Long-Range JetGlobal 7500, Gulfstream G65012–19$10,000 – $20,000+

A short regional hop in a light jet might run $10,000–$20,000 all-in; a transcontinental trip in a heavy or ultra-long-range jet can run well into six figures once positioning and overnight costs are added. These are industry-estimate ranges, not fixed quotes — the only way to know your actual number is to put a specific route and date to a broker. How much does cargo charter cost by aircraft category?

Cargo charter pricing follows the same mission-based logic but spans a far wider range, because the aircraft themselves span a far wider range — from a single-engine feeder carrying a few hundred kilograms to an Antonov An-124 carrying entire power-plant components.

Aircraft Category Example Aircraft Typical Payload Typical Hourly Rate (USD)
Small FeederCessna CaravanUp to 2 t$1,500 – $6,000
Narrowbody Freighter737-400SF, A321F18–25 t$8,000 – $28,000
Widebody Freighter767F, 777F50–100 t$25,000 – $90,000+
Outsize FreighterAntonov An-124Up to 120 t$70,000 – $110,000+

In practice, cargo charter is rarely discussed purely in per-hour terms — a full rotation (positioning flight out, load, flight to destination, return positioning) is what gets quoted as a single mission price. A short-haul narrowbody charter typically lands in the low tens of thousands of dollars all-in; a long-haul widebody mission scales into the mid-six figures; an An-124 outsize charter on a long intercontinental route can run into the high six figures and beyond.

What actually drives the final charter price?

Beyond the aircraft category itself, the same handful of factors move almost every charter quote, cargo or passenger:

  1. Positioning (ferry) flights. If the nearest available aircraft is not already near your departure airport, you pay for the empty flight to get it there — sometimes a meaningful share of the total quote.
  2. Landing, handling, and navigation fees. These vary enormously by airport and country, and DG or outsize cargo often adds specialized ground-handling costs (cranes, loaders, temperature-controlled storage) on top.
  3. Overflight and landing permits. International routings, particularly through multiple countries’ airspace, require permits that take time and money to secure — and some routes carry war-risk or high-risk-zone premiums on top.
  4. Fuel price on the day. Charter quotes are typically valid for a limited window because fuel costs move the underlying economics of the flight.
  5. Minimum block hours. Most operators quote a minimum number of flight hours per day regardless of actual flight time, which matters for short sectors.
  6. Seasonal and route demand. Hajj/Umrah season, major holidays, and e-commerce peak periods all push charter rates up on the routes and aircraft categories most affected — see our air charter brokerage guide for how a broker sources around this.
  7. Waiting and detention time. Delays at loading or customs beyond the scheduled window are typically billed separately from the base charter price.
  8. Empty legs. An aircraft already flying your route anyway, with no return cargo or passengers booked, can be chartered at a significant discount — see our empty leg flights guide for how that pricing works.
