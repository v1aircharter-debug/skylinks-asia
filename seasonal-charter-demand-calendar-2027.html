Air Charter Seasonal Demand Calendar 2027

Peaks, lulls, and when to book

By the Skylinks Asia Charter Desk · Published November 2026 · Planning year: 2027

TL;DR: Charter demand across Asia, the Middle East, and Africa follows a handful of predictable pressure points: the pre-Chinese New Year cargo rush (Chinese New Year falls on 6 February 2027), Ramadan (expected from about 8 February), Eid al-Fitr (about 9–10 March), Hajj and Eid al-Adha (about 14–17 May), Pakistan's mango export season (June to September), and the traditional Q4 retail peak. 2027 has an unusual feature: Chinese New Year and the start of Ramadan land within days of each other, so cargo and passenger demand overlap in early February. Islamic dates move roughly 10–11 days earlier each year and depend on moon sighting, so confirm exact dates close to travel. As a rule, book well ahead of any peak, and treat past price patterns with caution: 2026's peak season did not behave like earlier ones. Skylinks Asia arranges charter flights across all three regions and plans capacity around these cycles.

Why does seasonality matter in charter planning?

Charter pricing responds to local supply and demand on a given route and date, which is why a seasonal peak can raise rates and shrink the pool of available aircraft at once. The mission-pricing logic is covered in our charter cost guide.

What seasonality adds is predictability. Unlike an AOG breakdown or an emergency evacuation, most peaks below are known months in advance — which makes them the easiest kind of demand to plan around.

What happens when Chinese New Year and Ramadan land together in February 2027?

In 2026, Chinese New Year fell on 17 February, which analysts said stretched the traditional cargo cycle later into the first quarter. In 2027 it moves up to 6 February, so the pre-holiday cargo rush lands in January — and the post-holiday backlog arrives just as Ramadan starts around 8 February.

Passenger demand toward Saudi Arabia for Umrah builds at the same time cargo capacity across Asia is already tight. The practical effect is that early February 2027 may be harder to plan than a year earlier, particularly for anyone moving freight between China and the Gulf or South Asia, or arranging group travel to Saudi Arabia. Eid al-Fitr follows about a month later.

Should you assume the traditional Q4 peak still holds?

Q4 has long been the strongest quarter for air cargo, but 2026 showed that the textbook version no longer holds reliably. Xeneta's mid-year outlook pointed to a weaker second half and little sign of a traditional peak season, with freighter capacity being withdrawn from some China-to-Europe e-commerce services. Rates on corridors reshaped by the Middle East conflict stayed far above pre-conflict levels and unwound only slowly.

DHL's analysis described demand shifting toward semiconductors, AI-related products, advanced manufacturing, and data-center equipment, while new EU customs measures slowed e-commerce growth from China. Chinese low-value e-commerce exports were reported down 11% year on year in July 2026, even as overall global air cargo demand rose about 6% in August.

The takeaway for planning: the calendar still tells you when demand pressure is likely, but not how high rates will go. Use it for timing and lead times, not as a price forecast. For time-critical freight, our urgent cargo charter desk works on a per-mission basis when scheduled freight cannot meet the window.

What planning rules of thumb should you follow for 2027?

  • Book earlier than you think. Build in at least 3–4 weeks for routine charter in the current market, and more ahead of Hajj, Eid, or the February overlap.
  • Lock in recurring volume before the peak. If you have steady weekly volume, a block space agreement arranged ahead of a peak often beats chasing spot capacity in it.
  • Shift dates where you can. A day or two either side of a holiday peak can change availability and price more than any other lever.
  • Watch for empty legs on the return. Peaks create strongly one-directional flows, and the repositioning legs behind them can be priced attractively — see our empty leg guide.
  • Confirm Islamic dates close to travel. Moon sighting can move Ramadan, Eid, and Hajj dates by a day or two, so keep schedules flexible around them.
