Empty Leg Flights Singapore:
Price Guide and Live Routes (2026)

By the Skylinks Asia Aviation Team · Published November 2026

TL;DR: Empty leg flights from Singapore are one-way repositioning flights sold at 50% to 75% below standard private jet charter rates. Prices typically range from $3,500–$5,000 for light jets to Kuala Lumpur, $6,000–$9,000 for midsize jets to Bali or Bangkok, and $10,000–$15,000 for heavy jets to Tokyo or Hong Kong. They are published 48 hours to 14 days before departure and operate under the exact same AOC safety regulations as standard charters. To book one, you must remain flexible and request a quote immediately when an alert is issued.

Singapore is one of the busiest private aviation hubs in Asia. With a massive concentration of ultra-high-net-worth individuals, corporate headquarters, and a premier FBO at Seletar Airport (XSP) alongside Changi (SIN), private jets are constantly moving in and out of the city-state.

Because operators need to move their aircraft to pick up the next paying customer, they often fly empty. Instead of absorbing the fuel and crew costs of an empty flight, operators sell these one-way repositioning flights to the public as "empty legs" at steep discounts.

How much do empty leg flights from Singapore cost?

Pricing for empty leg flights from Singapore is entirely dependent on the aircraft class and the distance to the destination. Because these are one-way flights, you avoid the expensive positioning costs of a standard one-way charter.

What routes are most common for Singapore empty legs?

Because Singapore is an island city-state, empty legs are naturally one-way departures to regional destinations. The most common routes are heavily driven by corporate traffic and weekend leisure travel.

  • Singapore to Bali (DPS). A massive route for weekend getaways. Midsize jets frequently fly passengers to Bali and return empty on Sunday evenings or Monday mornings.
  • Singapore to Bangkok (BKK). Constant corporate and leisure traffic. Empty legs to Bangkok are frequent, particularly on Friday afternoons.
  • Singapore to Kuala Lumpur (KUL). The shortest and most common regional hop. Light jets often reposition here for maintenance or to pick up passengers heading to other parts of Malaysia.
  • Singapore to Hong Kong (HKG) or Tokyo (HND). These are less frequent but highly sought after. They usually occur when an operator is repositioning a heavy jet for a major charter event or a wealthy owner's itinerary.

If you are looking for routes outside of Singapore, see our full empty leg Asia guide.

How do I find and book an empty leg from Singapore?

Booking an empty leg requires speed and flexibility. You cannot plan an empty leg months in advance — they are strictly last-minute opportunities.

The best way to find them is to register for alerts. When an operator lists an empty leg, our system immediately notifies subscribers who have matching routes. Because empty legs are sold on a first-come, first-served basis, you must be ready to commit immediately.

When you request a quote, we will provide the aircraft type, operator name, registration, and confirmed departure time. Payment is usually required within a few hours of confirmation to secure the flight.

What aircraft types are used for Singapore empty legs?

Operators typically use aircraft that are already based in the region. For short regional flights to Malaysia or Indonesia, you will frequently see light and midsize jets. These aircraft are cost-effective to operate and perfect for 4 to 8 passengers.

For longer routes to Japan or South Korea, operators reposition heavy jets. These aircraft offer greater range, faster cruising speeds, and spacious cabins with full flat-bed seats. You can explore the full range of available aircraft on our private jet fleet page.

Why do empty legs exist and are they safe?

Empty legs exist strictly for logistical reasons. Operators are not allowed to fly a commercial charter flight without a paying customer. If an aircraft drops a client in Singapore and the next client is waiting in Bali, the aircraft must fly to Bali empty unless someone buys the leg.

Are they safe? Yes, completely. An empty leg is operated by the exact same commercial operator (AOC holder), the same pilots, and the same aircraft as a standard charter flight. At Skylinks Asia, we vet every operator to the same high standards regardless of whether you are booking a $5,000 empty leg or a $50,000 full private jet charter. You will always receive the operator details and insurance confirmation before you pay.
