Unbroken Cold Chain Logistics

Protecting Efficacy, Ensuring Freshness

Temperature excursions cost pharmaceutical companies millions and can cost lives. We source cargo aircraft capable of supporting active cooling containers (like Envirotainer and CSafe) and provide expedited ground handling to minimize tarmac exposure. From mRNA vaccines requiring deep freeze to fresh tropical fruit, we maintain the cold chain from origin to destination.

Quick Answer

Cold chain air charter moves temperature-sensitive cargo — vaccines and biologics typically at +2°C to +8°C or deep-frozen down to -70°C — using active containers (Envirotainer RKN/RAP, CSafe) or passive insulated containers. The biggest cold-chain risk isn't the flight itself, it's ground time. A dedicated charter minimizes tarmac exposure versus routing through a scheduled network.

IATA Certification

What Is CEIV Pharma?

CEIV Pharma is IATA's certification program for pharmaceutical air logistics, established to give shippers a consistent way to verify that an airline, ground handler, or forwarder actually meets pharmaceutical cold-chain standards — not just claims to. It's often described as a "GDP-plus" standard: it incorporates Good Distribution Practice requirements used globally, WHO Annex 5 guidance, and IATA's own Temperature Control Regulations.

For a shipper, the practical takeaway is this: CEIV-certified partners have been independently audited on their facilities, equipment, and staff training specifically for temperature-sensitive cargo, and certified operations consistently report meaningfully fewer temperature excursions than non-certified counterparts.

Equipment Selection

Active vs. Passive Containers

Both container types show up in pharma and perishables logistics, and the right one depends on the shipment's risk tolerance and duration:

  • Active containers (Envirotainer RKN/RAP, CSafe) carry their own powered refrigeration or heating system, actively holding a set temperature regardless of ambient conditions. Standard for high-value biologics and vaccines.
  • passive containers rely on insulation and thermal mass — gel packs or phase-change materials, and for deep-frozen shipments, dry ice — to hold temperature without power. Lighter, cheaper, and suited to shorter transit times.

Dry Ice & Dangerous Goods Overlap

One detail worth flagging: dry ice used in passive containers is itself classified as a Class 9 dangerous good under IATA regulations because of the carbon dioxide gas it releases. Any cold chain shipment using dry ice needs to be handled under our dangerous goods charter protocols alongside the standard cold chain handling.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CEIV Pharma certification?

CEIV Pharma is an IATA certification verifying that an airline, ground handler, or forwarder's facilities, equipment, and staff meet pharmaceutical cold-chain standards, combining Good Distribution Practice requirements, WHO guidance, and IATA's own Temperature Control Regulations into a single audited standard.

What's the difference between active and passive cold chain containers?

Active containers, such as Envirotainer RKN/RAP or CSafe units, carry their own powered refrigeration and hold a set temperature regardless of external conditions. Passive containers rely on insulation and thermal mass, such as gel packs or dry ice, and suit shorter transit times or less sensitive cargo.

What temperature ranges can be maintained during air cargo charter?

Standard pharma cold chain typically covers +2°C to +8°C for most vaccines and biologics, with deep-freeze options down to around -20°C, and ultra-cold options as low as -70°C for certain mRNA vaccine products, depending on the container and aircraft configuration used.

Is dry ice regulated for cold chain air shipments?

Yes. Dry ice is classified as a Class 9 dangerous good under IATA regulations because of the carbon dioxide gas it releases in an enclosed space, and any shipment using it must follow dangerous goods handling requirements in addition to standard cold chain protocols.

Why does a dedicated charter reduce temperature excursion risk compared to scheduled cargo?

Most temperature excursions happen on the ground, not in the air — during tarmac transfers, connections, and customs delays common on a scheduled route. A dedicated charter minimizes those touchpoints and ground exposure time by controlling the full route and handling process directly.

Can the same charter handle both pharmaceutical and perishable cargo?

Often, yes, since both rely on similar temperature-controlled aircraft and container infrastructure, though pharma shipments typically require stricter documentation, monitoring, and CEIV-certified handling than general perishables like produce or flowers.

What happens if a temperature excursion occurs during a cold chain shipment?

A properly monitored shipment will flag the excursion via onboard data loggers, allowing the receiving party to assess whether the product's stability window was breached. Prevention through active containers, CEIV-certified handling, and minimized ground time is far preferable to managing an excursion after the fact.

How far in advance should a cold chain charter be booked?

Active container availability (Envirotainer, CSafe) and appropriately equipped aircraft should ideally be confirmed several days ahead for planned shipments, though genuinely urgent pharma or perishable moves can be prioritized through our urgent cargo charter desk.
