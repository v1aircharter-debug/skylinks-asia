When Scheduled Freight Isn't Fast Enough

Grounded passenger planes, missed connections, and production line shutdowns require immediate action. Our 24/7 charter desk mobilizes freighter aircraft within hours. From small turboprops for regional cross-border transport to widebody freighters for intercontinental supply chain emergencies, we bypass standard cargo schedules to deliver your critical freight directly to its destination. If your time-critical freight involves hazardous materials, we are fully equipped for Dangerous Goods (DGR) transport, and for temperature-sensitive shipments, we offer rapid cold-chain pharma logistics.

Quick answer: For time-critical freight, you generally have two options — a next-flight-out (NFO) booking on the nearest available scheduled flight, or a dedicated cargo charter that flies on your timeline alone. AOG (Aircraft on Ground) situations, where a grounded aircraft is losing revenue every hour, are the most common trigger for urgent charter, alongside production line shutdowns and critical energy-infrastructure failures. A dedicated charter typically gets critical freight door-to-tarmac in a matter of hours once an aircraft is sourced, versus a day or more for standard freight routing.

What Does "AOG" Actually Mean, and Why Does It Need a Charter?

AOG — Aircraft on Ground — is the term used across the industry when an aircraft is grounded and unable to fly until a specific technical issue is resolved, whether that's a missing part, a required repair, or a compliance hold. It's treated as an emergency because the cost compounds by the hour: industry estimates commonly cite $10,000 to $150,000 in lost revenue per hour of downtime, depending on aircraft type and route, before accounting for cascading effects like missed connections, crew disruption, and contractual penalties.

That cost structure is exactly why AOG situations justify paying a premium for speed. Waiting on standard freight routing to move a part that might sit in a warehouse for days isn't a realistic option when every hour of grounding has a five- or six-figure price tag attached.

Next Flight Out vs. Dedicated Charter: Which Do You Need?

Not every urgent shipment needs a full aircraft charter. Two options typically apply, and knowing the difference helps you brief our desk faster:

  • Next Flight Out (NFO). Your freight is booked onto the soonest available scheduled flight with cargo space, often the fastest option when a suitable flight already exists on your route within the next few hours.
  • Dedicated cargo charter. An entire aircraft is sourced and flown on your schedule alone — the right call when no scheduled flight lines up in time, when the shipment is oversized or irregular, or when the routing itself (a remote site, a non-hub airport) isn't served by scheduled cargo at all.

Our charter desk assesses both options in parallel the moment you call, rather than defaulting to whichever is easier to book — the goal is the fastest realistic option for your specific deadline, not just the first one available.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does AOG mean in aviation?

AOG stands for Aircraft on Ground — an aircraft that's grounded and unable to fly until a specific technical, maintenance, or compliance issue is resolved. It's one of the most costly disruptions in aviation because the aircraft generates no revenue while it sits idle.

How fast can an urgent or AOG cargo charter be arranged?

Once aircraft availability and permits are confirmed, a dedicated charter can typically get critical freight door-to-tarmac within a matter of hours for regional routes, and generally under a day for intercontinental moves — considerably faster than standard freight routing, which can take a day or more.

What's the difference between next-flight-out (NFO) and a dedicated charter?

NFO books your shipment onto the soonest available scheduled flight with cargo space — fast when a suitable flight already exists. A dedicated charter reserves an entire aircraft flown on your schedule alone, needed when no scheduled flight fits your deadline, the shipment is oversized, or the destination isn't served by scheduled cargo at all.

How much does an AOG situation actually cost an operator?

Industry estimates commonly cite $10,000 to $150,000 in lost revenue per hour of aircraft downtime, depending on aircraft type and route, before factoring in cascading costs like missed connections, crew disruption, and contractual penalties — which is why urgent charter is priced at a premium but frequently pays for itself many times over.

Can urgent charter handle dangerous goods or hazardous aircraft parts?

Yes. Many AOG parts — batteries, hydraulic fluids, and certain avionics components — are classified as dangerous goods under IATA regulations. Our charter desk handles the classification and compliance requirements, including shipments that must move Cargo Aircraft Only.

What industries most commonly need urgent air charter?

Aerospace and airline operators (AOG parts), automotive and electronics manufacturers (production line resupply), and energy and utilities operators (transformer and grid-component failures) are the most consistent sources of urgent charter demand, alongside occasional emergency passenger group movements.

Can I book an urgent charter outside normal business hours?

Yes — our charter desk operates 24/7 specifically because AOG and production emergencies don't happen on a schedule. Emergency requests are typically actioned immediately regardless of time zone or time of day.
