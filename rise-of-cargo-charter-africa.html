The Rise of Cargo Charter in Africa

August 2024 • Skylinks Asia Team

For decades, the African continent has been plagued by a logistical paradox. It is home to some of the world's fastest-growing economies and richest mineral reserves, yet it suffers from a severe deficit in transportation infrastructure. Poor road networks, congested ports, and unreliable rail systems have historically stifled trade. However, a silent revolution is taking place in the skies. The demand for on-demand cargo aircraft charter is experiencing unprecedented growth across Africa. From the tech hubs of Nairobi to the mining belts of the DRC, freighter charter is bridging the infrastructure gap and keeping African supply chains moving.

The E-Commerce Boom and the Middle Class

One of the primary drivers of this shift is the explosive growth of African e-commerce. With a rapidly expanding middle class and increasing smartphone penetration, platforms like Jumia and Kilimall are moving millions of parcels daily. Traditional postal services cannot handle this volume. Furthermore, consumers expect delivery times that mirror Western standards.

This has led to a massive surge in demand for narrow-body freighters, such as the Boeing 737-800BCF and the ATR 72F. These aircraft are chartered to fly consolidated e-commerce loads between major hubs like Lagos (LOS), Nairobi (NBO), and Addis Ababa (ADD) overnight. The speed and reliability of dedicated air charter allow e-commerce giants to bypass clogged seaports and broken road networks entirely.

The Oil, Gas, and Mining Lifeline

While e-commerce drives volume, the extractive industries drive heavy lift. Africa is the world’s leading source of cobalt (DRC), a critical component for EV batteries, and a major producer of copper, gold, and diamonds.

Moving a 30-ton Caterpillar excavator or a massive mine-duty truck to a remote site in the bush cannot be done by road—there are no roads. This requires the deployment of heavy ramp-loading turboprop aircraft like the Ilyushin Il-76 or Antonov An-12. These aircraft are essentially flying flatbed trucks, capable of landing on compacted dirt strips and offloading heavy machinery directly onto the soil via their rear cargo ramps.

Humanitarian Relief and Outbreak Response

Africa is unfortunately no stranger to humanitarian crises, from natural disasters to infectious disease outbreaks. When an Ebola or Cholera outbreak occurs in a remote region, time is of the essence. Commercial airlines will immediately suspend flights to affected areas due to health risks.

Cargo charters become the only lifeline. Dedicated freighters are chartered by NGOs and the UN to fly in field hospitals, heavy medical equipment, and temperature-sensitive vaccines. Because these flights operate outside commercial schedules, they can be airborne within 12 hours of an emergency declaration, a speed that saves lives.

The Supply Chain Bottleneck: Bellies vs. Freighters

One might ask, why not just use the cargo belly of commercial passenger flights? The answer lies in capacity and routing. Commercial belly cargo is restricted by passenger schedules and baggage loads. If a passenger flight is full, the cargo gets bumped.

Furthermore, commercial airlines only fly between major capital cities. If a manufacturer in Johannesburg needs to send critical mining spares to Kolwezi (FKZW) in the DRC, there are no commercial options. A dedicated cargo charter is the only solution. It flies directly from origin to destination, without transiting through congested mega-hubs, minimizing the risk of loss or delay.
