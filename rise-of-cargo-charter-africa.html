The Rise of Cargo Charter in Africa

Updated 8 November 2026 · Skylinks Asia Team

TL;DR: Cargo charter flights have become the backbone of time-critical logistics across Africa. E-commerce platforms charter narrow-body freighters overnight between Lagos, Nairobi and Addis Ababa; mining and oil and gas operators use ramp-loading aircraft such as the Ilyushin Il-76 to reach dirt strips no scheduled carrier serves; and NGOs rely on dedicated freighters when outbreaks shut commercial routes. Charter costs typically run $18,000 to $35,000 per block hour for a narrow-body freighter and $30,000 to $60,000 for heavy-lift or wide-body aircraft, depending on route, payload and permits. This guide explains the demand drivers, the aircraft used, the permit landscape and how to source capacity in under 48 hours.

Cargo charter flights now move a disproportionate share of Africa's urgent, oversized and temperature-sensitive freight. The continent holds roughly 30% of global mineral reserves, yet its road and rail networks remain thin. Dedicated freighters fill the gap: they fly point-to-point, bypass congested ports, and reach airstrips that scheduled airlines will never serve. This guide explains the demand drivers, the aircraft used, the permit landscape, and how to source capacity quickly.

What is driving demand for cargo charter in Africa?

Three forces are compounding. First, e-commerce volumes are growing faster than postal and road infrastructure can absorb. Second, mining and oil and gas operators need heavy equipment delivered to sites with no road access. Third, humanitarian agencies need guaranteed lift when commercial airlines suspend routes during outbreaks. Each driver pulls a different aircraft type into the market, which is why African cargo charter demand is broad rather than cyclical. Our urgent cargo charter services cover the operational side of each scenario.

How is e-commerce changing African air freight?

African e-commerce is growing at roughly 15 to 20% a year, and platforms such as Jumia and Kilimall now move millions of parcels monthly. Traditional postal networks cannot handle that volume, and consumers increasingly expect delivery times that mirror Western standards.

Brokers are chartering Boeing 737-800BCF and ATR 72F freighters for overnight runs between Lagos (LOS), Nairobi (NBO) and Addis Ababa (ADD). These aircraft carry 8 to 23 tonnes, fly on schedules the integrators control, and bypass seaports and border posts entirely.

Why do mining and oil and gas operators rely on cargo charters?

The Democratic Republic of Congo produces roughly 70% of the world's cobalt, and copper, gold and diamond operations are spread across remote terrain. A 30-tonne excavator cannot travel by road to a site with no road.

Operators charter ramp-loading aircraft such as the Ilyushin Il-76 or Antonov An-12, which land on compacted dirt strips and offload directly via rear ramps. The same pattern applies to rig moves and crew rotations in oil and gas fields. Our oil and gas air cargo desk handles both.

How does cargo charter support humanitarian and outbreak response?

When Ebola or cholera outbreaks occur, commercial airlines suspend service within days due to health risks. Dedicated freighters become the only reliable lift.

NGOs and UN agencies charter aircraft to move field hospitals, medical equipment and temperature-controlled vaccines. Because charters operate outside commercial schedules, an aircraft can be airborne within 12 to 24 hours of an emergency declaration. Speed is the product. Our humanitarian relief flights desk runs these missions regularly.

Why choose a freighter over passenger belly cargo?

Belly cargo is constrained by passenger schedules, baggage loads and routing. If a passenger flight is full, freight gets bumped. Commercial airlines also only serve major capitals.

A manufacturer in Johannesburg sending critical mining spares to Kolwezi (FKZW) in the DRC has no scheduled option. A dedicated freighter flies direct, without transiting congested mega-hubs, cutting handling risk and transit time.

What permits and regulations apply to African cargo charters?

Every cross-border charter needs overflight and landing permits, and timelines vary from 24 hours to two weeks. Cabotage rules restrict foreign operators moving cargo between two points inside the same country. Diplomatic clearance is required for certain cargo, including weapons, hazardous goods and some humanitarian shipments. Dangerous goods must comply with IATA DGR. The practical sequence is:

  1. Confirm payload, dimensions and destination strip capability.
  2. Secure overflight and landing permits for each sovereign airspace.
  3. Verify the operator's AOC and insurance for the specific route.
  4. Confirm ground handling, fuel and loading equipment at the destination.
  5. Execute with 24/7 flight-following and contingency planning.

Why work with a charter broker for African cargo?

Operating in African airspace requires route-level knowledge: which AOC holders are approved for which countries, which strips can accept which aircraft, and how long each permit authority actually takes. A single delayed diplomatic clearance can ground a $50,000 flight for days.
